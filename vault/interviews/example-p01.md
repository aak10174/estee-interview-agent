# P01

## Summary
P01 is a 22-year-old university student in Shanghai who discovers products through peer validation on Xiaohongshu rather than advertising. She is price-flexible but risk-averse, willing to spend significantly when she has trusted proof (like a friend's product match) but resistant to committing money based on photos alone. She currently favors Chinese brands like Florasis and Proya for their affordability, transparency, and direct communication style, and views Estée Lauder as her mother's legacy brand rather than something for her.

## Brand perception
- **Estée Lauder — mixed — Seen as good quality but generationally distant and intimidating in presentation**
  > It's my mum's brand. The brown bottle, everyone knows it. It's good quality I think, but it feels like something you get given, not something you choose. When I see the counter it looks like a bank.
- **Proya — positive — Affordable, transparent ingredient labeling, personable communication**
  > Proya is like a third of the price and the ingredient list is right on the front. And they actually talk to you like a person online.
- **Florasis — positive — Current go-to for makeup, mentioned alongside Proya as preferred domestic option**
  > Mostly Chinese brands now — Florasis for makeup, Proya for skincare.
- **Lancôme — positive — Willing to pay premium price after trusted trial via friend**
  > I spent 600 yuan on a Lancôme foundation last year because a friend let me try hers and it matched.

## Unmet needs
- **Risk-free trial before committing to prestige purchase**
  > What I won't do is pay 600 for something I've only seen in a photo. The risk is the problem, not the price.
- **A brand identity distinct from her mother's generation**
  > If they made something that was obviously for me and not for my mum.
- **Sampling access to reduce purchase risk**
  > And samples. If I could try it without committing, honestly I'd probably buy it.

## Discovery & purchase channels
- **Xiaohongshu — discover**
  > Honestly Xiaohongshu. I don't watch ads. If three different people I follow post about the same serum in one week I'll look it up.
- **Department store counter — purchase**
  > My mum buys from the counter at the department store but I've never bought anything that way.
- **Friend recommendation/trial — all**
  > I spent 600 yuan on a Lancôme foundation last year because a friend let me try hers and it matched.

## Purchase drivers
- **Peer social proof / repeated organic mentions**
  > If three different people I follow post about the same serum in one week I'll look it up.
- **Certainty of product fit before spending**
  > I'll pay if I'm sure.
- **Ingredient transparency**
  > the ingredient list is right on the front

## Purchase barriers
- **Perceived risk of unproven products, especially at high price**
  > What I won't do is pay 600 for something I've only seen in a photo. The risk is the problem, not the price.
- **Brand association with older generation, feels imposed rather than chosen**
  > it feels like something you get given, not something you choose
- **Intimidating retail environment**
  > When I see the counter it looks like a bank.

## Product preferences
- **Transparent ingredient labeling**
  > the ingredient list is right on the front
- **Products validated by trusted peers before purchase**
  > a friend let me try hers and it matched

## Competitors chosen instead
- **Proya — Lower price, transparent labeling, personable brand communication**
  > Proya is like a third of the price and the ingredient list is right on the front. And they actually talk to you like a person online.
- **Florasis — Preferred domestic makeup brand**
  > Mostly Chinese brands now — Florasis for makeup, Proya for skincare.
- **Lancôme — Trusted via friend's trial, confirmed product match before purchase**
  > I spent 600 yuan on a Lancôme foundation last year because a friend let me try hers and it matched.

## Price sensitivity
**medium**
> Not exactly. I spent 600 yuan on a Lancôme foundation last year because a friend let me try hers and it matched. I'll pay if I'm sure.

## Tensions & contradictions
- She claims price isn't the main factor ('Not exactly') yet immediately cites Proya's lower cost as a key reason she switched from prestige brands, showing price still plays a role despite her framing it as secondary to trust/risk.
- She dismisses ads and counter shopping entirely, yet her big prestige purchase (Lancôme) still came through an indirect brand-touch experience via a friend, suggesting trial/experience remains critical even in a digital-first discovery model.

## Notable quotes
- It's my mum's brand. The brown bottle, everyone knows it.
- When I see the counter it looks like a bank.
- The risk is the problem, not the price.
- If they made something that was obviously for me and not for my mum. And samples. If I could try it without committing, honestly I'd probably buy it.

## Decision signals
- **Attracting Gen Z:** Gen Z consumers like P01 are drawn to brands that feel personally chosen rather than inherited, communicate directly and personably online, and offer transparency (e.g., visible ingredients). Legacy brand equity can read as distance from their generation rather than trust.
- **Channel investment:** Xiaohongshu-style peer/organic social content carries far more discovery weight than traditional advertising or counter retail; repeated organic mentions from multiple trusted accounts are a key trigger for research.
- **Portfolio direction:** There is an opportunity for sampling programs or trial mechanisms to reduce perceived risk, plus sub-brand or product lines that feel distinctly youth-oriented rather than shared with an older generational image.
